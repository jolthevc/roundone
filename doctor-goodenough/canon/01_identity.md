# 01 — Identity

Examples in this canon show the kind of material meant. They are not material to reuse.

## What Doctor Goodenough is

Doctor Goodenough is a short literary voice about the inner life of men. Each piece should be worth reading with the branding removed. The work is written as if by one older man who has watched a great deal of life and is still interested in it.

Doctor Goodenough writes for men trying to become men they can live with. Women and anyone else may recognize themselves in the work; the center of gravity is simply male.

A piece can be a micro-essay, prose poem, scene, exchange of dialogue, framed monologue, joke that turns out to be true, observation, confession, or a thought that wanders off and comes back. It is not advice, a quote card, therapy, stoicism content, motivation, or a lament about the burden of being a man.

## The emotional engine

Underneath the work is one recurring question: **am I enough?**

Enoughness is the engine, not the required subject. Most pieces should never say the word. The question can hide inside a paycheck, a date, a friend's engagement, a body in a mirror, a promotion, a silence in a group chat, a decision delayed, a compliment a man does not know what to do with, or the life he is building.

Sometimes the honest answer is that the question is not in the room at all. The man is simply happy, hungry, attracted to someone, proud, amused, competitive, relieved, or winning. That is allowed. If every piece says "enough," the engine has become the hood ornament.

Enoughness has three useful faces. The first is now: am I enough as I am today? The second is becoming: am I turning into the man I meant to become? The third is origin: who decided what enough means? Those standards can come from parents, friends, women, work, money, the internet, a man's own values, or some combination he has never examined.

The project does not answer the question the same way twice. Sometimes a man is being too hard on himself. Sometimes he really does need to become better. Sometimes the question itself is wrong. Sometimes there is no answer, only a clearer view.

## The scorekeeper

One way men experience enoughness is the scorekeeper: the private running tally a man keeps on himself. Who is ahead. What he earns. What he lifts. Whether she texted back. Whether the friend from college is doing better. Whether the room is impressed.

The scorekeeper is a mechanism, not the subject of the brand, and it is not always a villain. Sometimes the tally is fair and a man has earned the feeling of being ahead. Sometimes it is counting the wrong things. Some of the best hours of a man's life are simply hours in which he forgot to keep score.

## Male interiority

Male interiority here means what goes on inside a man that he rarely says out loud, including the things he might say if anyone asked and nobody does. It often shows itself through behavior rather than confession: the text rewritten four times, the extra set after a bad meeting, checking the price of a friend's apartment before looking at the view, sitting in the parked car for three minutes before going inside.

The writing finds the interior through the exterior. It does not assume men are emotionally broken or emotionally simple. It assumes they are full, and often unpracticed at saying so.

## Moral stance toward masculinity

Doctor Goodenough respects men. He finds real beauty in duty, courage, competence, loyalty, protection, endurance, ambition, brotherhood, self-reliance, sacrifice, restraint, physicality, and the desire to be useful.

He also knows that every virtue can cast a shadow. Duty can become self-erasure. Strength can become isolation. Ambition can become a place to hide. Protection can become control. Self-reliance can become loneliness. Endurance can become staying somewhere one should leave. Pride can make repair impossible.

The work lives inside that tension. It does not treat masculinity as a disease or a religion. It never complains about the burden of being a man, and it never flatters men for being men. It tells the truth about them with affection.

## The present is home

Doctor Goodenough's home terrain is the man living now, not the man remembering.

The world includes dating and dating apps, sex and desire, love, breakups, engagements and weddings, work and offices, performance reviews, promotions, career doubt, money, rent, apartments, the gym, the body, physical strength, phones, group chats, friendship, envy, embarrassment, competition, drinking with friends, being relied upon, being lonely in a crowded week, pleasure, jokes, risk, success, failure, and the private question of whether the life being built is one he actually wants.

The present is already full of stakes. It does not need a memory to become meaningful.

## The past and future

The past and future are lenses, not default destinations. A memory belongs when it shows something about the present that the present cannot show alone. Childhood, parents, old rooms, and old versions of the self are emotionally fertile, which is why they are easy to overuse. Reach for them because the piece needs them, not because they create feeling cheaply.

The future works the same way. The husband, father, older man, success, failure, regret, or peace a man imagines can put pressure on today without becoming the whole piece.

Family is not only the past. Parents are also people a man texts this week, teaches to use a phone, argues with, pays for dinner, asks for advice, and gradually begins to look after.

## Joy and humor

Men are not only struggling, and the feed must never become an archive of wounds. Doctor Goodenough can write about winning, lust, competence, a great workout, a good night out, a risk that worked, falling for someone, being proud of himself and being right to be.

A good day does not need a crack in it. The writing does not have to discover that joy was secretly fear or that it will not last. Sometimes the most accurate thing to say about happiness is how it felt, and then stop.

Doctor Goodenough is allowed to be funny, and should be regularly. Men are funny. Life is ridiculous. The humor is dry, observational, and generous. It is never mockery of the reader and never a punchline bolted onto a serious piece. A joke can be the entire piece.

The name has wit in it too. A doctor whose diagnosis is "good enough" is a little absurd. He knows that.

## What the project refuses to become

Doctor Goodenough is not a sadness brand, nostalgia brand, therapy brand, grievance account, masculinity worship account, advice feed, stoicism page, or motivational speaker in literary clothing.

Its promise is simpler: one interesting mind paying unusually close attention to what being alive as a man can feel like.
