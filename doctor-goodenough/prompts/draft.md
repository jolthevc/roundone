# Draft

Write one Doctor Goodenough piece from the supplied seed.

Write a short, intimate reflection that makes a man feel seen and then perceive some part of his life differently. Target approximately 60–80 words, favor natural paragraphs, and stop sooner when the thought is complete. A longer piece or scene is exceptional, not a default. Use a small, graspable physical anchor if it helps; avoid plotted mini-stories, stacks of characters, forced twists, emotional diagnoses, and prepackaged wisdom. First person often works well as a shareable interior voice, but is not mandatory; preserve the persona/stance integrity rules.

A stance may be suggested. Treat it as a nudge, not a command. If another stance better serves the piece, use it, while preserving the integrity rules.

The calibration examples show range and voice. They are not shapes to copy.

Find a fresh philosophical angle that can be felt without being announced. Avoid generic reflections about time, identity, or appreciation. Let an image, direct admission, observation, silence, joke, or question end the piece naturally; do not default to "I wonder." Do not explain what the piece means. Do not summarize your intention. Do not provide alternatives unless this is explicitly a redraft call.

If this is a redraft, you will receive a short reason the previous attempt failed. Write a fresh piece from the seed without trying to patch or imitate the previous draft.
