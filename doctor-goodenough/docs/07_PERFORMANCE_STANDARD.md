# 07 — Performance Standard

## Two canonical artifacts

Every finished piece has two related but distinct versions.

### Page copy

The literary source of truth.

This is what appears visually.

### Performance script

A delivery score created from the locked page copy.

This is what the narrator receives.

The performance pass may change delivery.

It may not casually change meaning or rewrite the literature.

## Why the distinction matters

Writing that is beautiful on a page can become confusing when heard once.

Writing that sounds natural aloud can look visually clumsy.

Doctor Goodenough is written for both the eye and the ear.

Neither is subordinate to the other.

## Read-aloud test

Before page copy is locked, read it aloud at Doctor Goodenough's natural pace.

Listen for sentences that run too long, syntax that depends too heavily on visible punctuation, awkward word clusters, accidental tongue-twisters, monotonous sentence lengths, turns that need more air, lines that look beautiful but die when spoken, and places where weak writing is asking performance to rescue it.

If the prose itself does not speak well, revise the prose before over-directing the narrator.

## Performance adaptation

The performance script may add line breaks, add pauses, separate clauses into spoken beats, alter speech-only punctuation, indicate a slight change of pace or emotional temperature, or clarify pronunciation when genuinely needed.

It should preserve the words whenever possible.

Example page copy:

> Maybe the hardest part isn't starting over. It's knowing how far there is to go.

Possible performance script:

> [quiet]  
> Maybe the hardest part... isn't starting over.  
> [pause]  
> It's knowing how far there is to go.

Same language.

Different time.

## Direction vocabulary

Keep stage directions sparse.

Preferred vocabulary:
- [quiet]
- [short pause]
- [pause]
- [long pause]
- [slower]
- [quieter]
- [closer]
- [firmer]
- [warmly]
- [dry]
- [let it land]

This list is not sacred. Restraint is.

A 130-word piece should not contain twenty-five stage directions.

Doctor Goodenough's default performance already carries most of the character.

## Silence

Silence is part of the performance.

A pause can allow recognition, separate thought from discovery, prevent sentimentality, or let a final line exist without explanation.

Do not pause dramatically after every important sentence.

## Performance principle

> **The literature is the source of truth. The performance script is its score.**
