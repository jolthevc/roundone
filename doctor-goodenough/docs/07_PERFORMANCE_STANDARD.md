# 07 — Performance Standard

## Two canonical artifacts

Every finished piece has two related but distinct versions.

### Page copy

The literary source of truth.

This is what the audience sees.

### Performance script

A delivery score created from the locked page copy.

This is what the narrator receives.

The performance pass may change delivery.

It may not casually change meaning or rewrite the literature.

## The voice should know the subject

Doctor Goodenough should sound like a man who recognizes the struggle from the inside.

Not a narrator reading "content for men."

Not a therapist speaking gently at a patient.

Not a coach delivering a lesson.

He sounds like he has been scared, proud, foolish, lonely, ambitious, loved, angry, wrong, useful, and unsure before.

That lived texture should be heard in restraint, not dramatized biography.

## Read-aloud test

Before page copy is locked, read it aloud at Doctor Goodenough's natural pace.

Listen for:

- lines that look beautiful but sound artificial
- syntax that is hard to follow once
- forced line breaks
- accidental singsong rhythm
- long sentences that need air
- words the narrator would never naturally say
- places where performance is being asked to rescue weak writing

If the prose itself does not speak well, revise the prose.

## Performance adaptation

The performance script may:

- add line breaks
- add pauses
- separate clauses into spoken beats
- adjust speech-only punctuation
- indicate a slight change in pace or emotional temperature
- clarify pronunciation when needed

Preserve the words whenever possible.

## Direction vocabulary

Keep stage directions sparse.

Useful tags include:

- [quiet]
- [short pause]
- [pause]
- [long pause]
- [slower]
- [quieter]
- [closer]
- [firmer]
- [warmly]
- [dry]
- [let it land]

The list is not sacred.

Restraint is.

## Emotional calibration

Do not turn vulnerability into sadness by default.

A piece about a father may be warm.

A piece about failure may be dry.

A piece about childhood may contain joy before ache.

A piece about responsibility may sound steady rather than heavy.

A piece about anger may be controlled rather than aggressive.

## Silence

Silence is part of the writing once it becomes audio.

A pause may allow recognition, shame, tenderness, humor, or a final image to exist without explanation.

Do not pause dramatically after every important line.

## Performance principle

> **The literature is the source of truth. The performance script is its score.**
