There was a time when you came home from school  
and nobody asked what you were building.

You dropped your backpack by the door.  
Your mother gave you something to eat.  
You told her a story that went nowhere  
and laughed so hard you forgot the ending.

You were not useful yet.  
Not successful.  
Not impressive.

And somehow, nobody seemed worried.

A strange amount of adulthood is spent trying to earn back a feeling you once had for free:

that there was a chair at the table,

and you were already enough to sit in it.
