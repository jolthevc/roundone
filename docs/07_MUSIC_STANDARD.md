# 07 - Music Standard

## Role of music

Music is an emotional environment.

It is not a score.

The preferred behavior is:

**steady pressure under a changing narrator**

The music should make the words feel heavier without trying to tell the listener what happens next.

## Core test

The bed should be:

**almost invisible while playing, noticeably missing when muted.**

## What the music should create

Depending on the family:
- gravity
- tension
- introspection
- ache
- focus
- forward motion
- quiet menace
- unresolved feeling
- controlled intensity

## What the music should not do

Avoid:
- crescendos
- obvious climax
- victory sections
- heroic resolution
- trailer booms
- risers
- drops
- EDM behavior
- choir
- bright inspirational piano
- sentimental string swells
- busy percussion
- melodic soloing
- major structural transitions

The narrator owns the arc.

## Steady does not mean soft

A bed can feel intense without changing intensity.

We want:
- pressure
- low-end weight
- hypnotic repetition
- unresolved harmony
- restrained pulse
- negative space

A quiet bed can still feel dangerous.

## Preferred sonic language

Potential ingredients:
- repetitive low piano
- Rhodes or muted electric piano
- analog synth texture
- low sustained tones
- soft sub-bass
- restrained low strings
- processed saxophone
- minimal percussion
- tape-like texture
- faint industrial detail
- atmospheric noise

No single ingredient is mandatory.

Consistency comes from behavior, not instrumentation.

## Emotional ambiguity

The best reusable beds can work under:
- regret
- courage
- discipline
- doubt
- mortality
- ambition
- confrontation
- a quiet ending
- a shouted ending

The bed should communicate:

**something matters**

rather than:

**this is sad**
or
**this is triumphant**

## Reference principles

### Everything in Its Right Place - Radiohead
Borrow:
- hypnotic repetition
- unresolved harmonic feeling
- intensity without theatrical dynamics
- electronic atmosphere

Do not copy melody, harmony, arrangement, or production details.

### do i clench my fists? - ridgeclub
Borrow:
- negative space
- processed instrumental texture
- human imperfection
- minimal steady atmosphere

### Daredevil theme
Borrow:
- low repeating weight
- seriousness
- pulse
- disciplined darkness

Strip away the behavior of a television theme. We need a bed, not a title sequence.

### Red Sex - Vessel
Borrow selectively:
- physical pressure
- menace
- texture
- contained violence

Reduce foreground activity significantly.

### Jacob and the Stone - Emile Mosseri
Borrow:
- emotional weight
- beauty
- transcendence without obvious uplift

Avoid letting the music become emotionally directive.

### Je te laisserai des mots
Borrow:
- intimacy
- simplicity
- tenderness
- repetition

### Another Love - instrumental reference
Borrow:
- ache
- yearning
- emotional directness

Avoid recognizable song-like structure.

### On the Nature of Daylight - Max Richter
Borrow:
- gravity
- emotional seriousness
- sustained feeling

This reference is near the upper limit of how much emotion the bed should impose.

### Ominous reference clip
Borrow:
- dark inward-looking atmosphere
- emotional ambiguity
- steady background pressure
- usefulness under many kinds of narration

## Soundtrack families

Working families:

### 1. Pressure
Dark, low, unresolved, steady.

### 2. Gravitas
More spacious, reflective, often piano or textured keys.

### 3. Ache
Warmer, more human, restrained sadness or longing.

### 4. Resolve
Grounded and slightly warmer without becoming uplifting.

### 5. Sparse
Minimal texture, negative space, unusual timbre.

### 6. Hard
More physical low-end and pulse, but still no climax.

These are working creative families, not final product categories.

## Library philosophy

Prefer a small number of exceptional reusable beds.

Initial target:
approximately 5 to 8 masters.

They should feel like siblings, not copies.

Possible production model:
- 3 underlying musical systems
- 2 variations of each

## Human composer brief

Preferred instruction:

**Do not compose a six-minute story. Compose a six-minute emotional state.**

A master should:
- run long enough for typical Rounds
- have no required sync points
- avoid meaningful intro/outro behavior
- survive trimming
- remain coherent at random timestamps
- support looping if necessary
- be delivered with stems where possible

## Random-window test

Take any 30 to 45 second section.

Place it under:
- quiet story
- reflection
- hard truth
- aggressive ending

If one section feels emotionally wrong, the bed may be too narrative.

## Mix principle

The voice is always the lead instrument.

Mixing should:
- preserve intelligibility
- duck music subtly under speech where needed
- avoid obvious pumping
- preserve low-end weight without masking the voice
- permit a restrained fade in and fade out

Do not use loudness to make the music feel important.
