# 03 - Narrative Archetypes

These are internal creative scaffolds.

They are not listener-facing formats.
They are not templates to fill mechanically.
They should never become predictable.

The purpose of an archetype is to help a creator find the right vehicle for one idea.

## 1. The Scene

One cinematic moment carries the Round.

Best for:
- decisions
- pressure
- fear
- courage
- discipline
- human conflict

Shape:
- enter late
- stay close
- let details do work
- widen meaning only after the scene earns it

Failure mode:
The scene becomes decorative and nothing changes.

## 2. The True Story

A real person or event, focused on one meaningful moment.

Best for:
- resilience
- courage
- craft
- obsession
- overlooked decisions
- unlikely outcomes

Rule:
Research first.

Failure mode:
Turning a biography into a Wikipedia summary with motivational narration.

## 3. The Parable

A simple invented or traditional story that carries one realization.

Best for:
- timeless principles
- abstract ideas needing embodiment
- compact emotional arcs

Rule:
The story must be strong enough to survive without an explicit moral.

Failure mode:
A children's fable wearing expensive language.

## 4. The Parallel Parable

Two stories appear unrelated and converge.

Examples:
- a craftsman and an athlete
- a natural process and a human decision
- a historical scene and a present-day listener

Best for:
- surprise
- contrast
- layered realization

Failure mode:
The parallel is too clever and the emotional connection is weak.

## 5. Future Memory

The listener is placed in a possible future looking backward.

Best for:
- time
- regret
- mortality
- courage
- decision-making

Use carefully.

Failure mode:
Fear manipulation or melodrama.

The goal is not "you will die someday, panic."
The goal is "future perspective changes what this moment deserves."

## 6. Contrast

Two paths, people, choices, or outcomes are placed against each other.

Best for:
- discipline
- avoidance
- compounding
- identity
- courage

Failure mode:
One side becomes a cartoon villain or straw man.

## 7. Object

One physical object carries the piece.

Examples:
- stone
- hammer
- door
- match
- chair
- shoe
- clock
- key

Best for:
- simplicity
- visual callbacks
- memorable endings

Failure mode:
The object becomes a tortured metaphor with too many meanings.

## 8. Reframe

Something commonly interpreted as bad is reinterpreted.

Examples:
- fear
- failure
- rejection
- uncertainty
- slowness
- embarrassment

Best for:
- emotional reversal
- restoring agency

Failure mode:
Toxic positivity that denies real cost.

## 9. Process

The listener watches repetitive unseen work accumulate.

Examples:
- forging
- rehearsing
- training
- carving
- rebuilding
- practicing

Best for:
- patience
- compounding
- invisible progress
- craft

Failure mode:
Generic montage energy.

The details must be specific.

## 10. Confession / Reversal

The narrator inventories what appears to be failure, weakness, or waste, then turns the frame.

Best for:
- shame
- regret
- self-forgiveness
- renewed action

Failure mode:
Therapy monologue without narrative movement.

## 11. Origin

Return to the period before the outcome was obvious.

Best for:
- famous success stories
- identity
- uncertainty
- courage before validation

Failure mode:
Retrospective inevitability.

The whole point is that the person did not know how it would end.

## 12. Reveal

The listener discovers that the setup meant something different than expected.

Best for:
- perspective shifts
- hidden assumptions
- strong callbacks

Failure mode:
A gimmick twist.

The reveal must deepen the idea rather than merely surprise.

## Distribution principle

Do not let one archetype dominate the catalog.

A library full of parables will feel manufactured.
A library full of true stories will feel like a biography podcast.
A library full of future-regret pieces will feel manipulative.

Variety protects the magic.

## Selection question

Choose the archetype by asking:

**What vehicle makes this truth easiest to feel without having to explain it?**
