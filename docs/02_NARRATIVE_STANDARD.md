# 02 - Narrative Standard

This is the central creative document for ROUND ONE.

A Round succeeds because the story earns the meaning.

The listener should never feel that a generic motivational message was written first and a story was pasted around it afterward.

## Core architecture

**Story -> realization -> fire.**

A strong Round often passes through curiosity, identification, tension, discomfort, realization, possibility, conviction, and release.

Not every Round needs every beat explicitly.

Emotional movement matters more than structural obedience.

## One core idea

Every Round must answer:

**What is the one thing this piece is really saying?**

If the answer contains three unrelated lessons, the concept is not ready.

Examples:
- invisible work still counts
- fear is evidence that something matters
- failure does not remove your right to re-enter
- time already wasted is not permission to waste the rest
- progress rarely announces itself while it is happening
- your next action matters more than your last explanation

The core idea should be specific enough to create a story and broad enough to matter beyond it.

## Concept before script

Do not begin with prose.

A concept should first define:
- the truth
- the story vehicle
- the emotional valley
- the reversal
- the ending opportunity

If those are weak, beautiful sentences will only hide the problem.

## Opening standard

The story should begin within roughly 10 to 20 seconds.

Default rule:

**Concrete first. Meaning second.**

Good openings tend to give us a person, place, object, action, decision, physical detail, or question created by the scene.

Avoid opening with dictionary definitions, abstract philosophy, "Have you ever wondered...", a thesis statement, generic advice, throat-clearing, biography summary, or motivational declaration.

The opening should make the listener want the next sentence before they know the lesson.

## The story must move

A Round is not a stack of good lines.

Something must change.

Possible movement:
- an object breaks
- a person chooses
- a hidden fact is revealed
- a pattern accumulates
- a false interpretation flips
- two outcomes diverge
- a feared moment arrives
- the listener realizes what the story has been about

The story should create pressure rather than merely decorate an idea.

## Scene specificity

Use details that orient the listener, increase tension, reveal character, create image, or become useful later.

Do not include detail only to sound cinematic.

A detail earns its place when removing it weakens understanding, emotion, rhythm, callback, or meaning.

## The valley

Most strong Rounds need a moment where the easy interpretation stops working.

This can be the apparent failure, the cost, the humiliation, the realization of time lost, the moment the listener sees their own behavior in the story, the possibility that the desired outcome may never come, or the uncomfortable truth beneath the surface lesson.

The valley is not misery for its own sake.

Its job is to make the eventual reversal earned.

## The realization

The realization should feel discovered, not announced.

Ideal behavior:

The listener understands it a few seconds before the narrator fully states it.

The narrator then crystallizes the realization in language worth remembering.

Avoid:
- "And the moral of the story is..."
- explaining every symbol
- repeating the point three times
- making the listener feel slow
- turning ambiguity into a lecture

Rule:

**If the metaphor needs a paragraph of explanation, the metaphor is not good enough.**

## Reversal

Many ROUND ONE pieces benefit from reversal.

Examples:
- wasted time -> you still have time
- failure -> you survived the attempt
- fear -> the thing matters
- uncertainty -> no one gets certainty first
- invisibility -> unseen work is still work
- mortality -> this morning matters
- being behind -> you are still in the race
- rejection -> you now know what you are willing to risk

The reversal should not erase the pain.

It should reinterpret what the pain means.

## Micro-realizations

A Round should not save all value for one final twist.

Throughout the piece, include smaller moments that sharpen the listener's perception.

The piece should reward attention throughout.

## Memorable line standard

Every Round should contain at least one line that could survive outside the piece.

It should not sound engineered for social media.

Good memorable lines are usually simple, specific, rhythmically clean, connected to the story, and difficult to paraphrase without making them worse.

Avoid fortune-cookie writing.

## Turn toward the listener

The story earns the right to become personal.

Do not diagnose the listener immediately.

Preferred progression:
- watch something happen
- recognize what it means
- realize it also applies here
- turn the camera toward the listener

The listener should feel seen, not targeted.

## Full-circle endings

Callbacks are strongly preferred when natural.

The opening image, object, or action can return as the final command.

Examples:
- empty gym / lights off -> get there first
- door -> open the door
- hammer and stone -> one more hit
- bell -> answer it
- match -> strike it
- chair -> stand up

The callback should feel inevitable, not clever.

## The fire

The final 60 to 90 seconds are held to a higher standard.

This is where the Round stops merely interpreting and starts activating.

The fire can include direct address, shorter sentences, repetition, increased pace, profanity, sharper verbs, escalating conviction, and a clear behavioral challenge.

But the fire must emerge from the story.

Do not bolt a generic motivational speech onto the end.

The listener should feel:

**Of course this is where that story was going.**

## Profanity in the fire

Profanity can create force when it arrives after restraint, the narrator has earned intimacy, the word improves rhythm, the emotional register needs rupture, or the line would be weaker sanitized.

Profanity fails when it is constant, substitutes for specificity, sounds juvenile, attacks identity, or creates parody.

Good:
"You've been fucking around."

Bad:
"You're fucking pathetic."

Attack behavior.
Protect worth.

## Ending shape

A strong default pattern:

**conviction -> acceleration -> explosion -> quiet final line**

The last line can be quieter than everything before it.

That contrast often creates more power than another shouted sentence.

## Runtime and word count

Typical target:
- 5 to 7 minutes
- roughly 700 to 1,000 words depending on performance style
- slower voices may require less
- aggressive pieces may use shorter sentences and more pauses

Runtime is a constraint, not a quality metric.

Do not pad.

## True stories

If a Round uses a real person, event, historical moment, quotation, scientific claim, or statistic:
- research it
- source it
- distinguish known fact from inference
- do not fabricate dialogue
- do not invent athlete anecdotes
- do not use apocryphal quotes because they sound good
- do not compress events in a misleading way

The narrative can be cinematic without becoming false.

## Biography rule

A true-story Round is not a biography.

Choose one meaningful moment.

We rarely need birth date, childhood summary, career chronology, every setback, or every achievement.

The story is the moment that carries the idea.

## Simplicity

Premium does not mean complicated.

The best stories are often almost child-simple:
- a stone
- a hammer
- a door
- a race
- a match
- a person waiting
- a light left on
- one decision

Execution creates depth.

Complexity does not.

## Cliche blacklist

Use extreme caution with:
- lions
- wolves
- kings
- crowns
- beast mode
- "they slept on you"
- "prove them wrong" as the entire motivation
- "no excuses"
- mountains as default struggle imagery
- generic sunrise metaphors
- "the only person stopping you is you"
- "pain is weakness leaving the body"
- "you have 24 hours just like everyone else"

A cliche may appear only if transformed enough to feel newly true.

## Pseudo-philosophy blacklist

Reject writing that sounds deep because it is abstract.

Warning signs:
- nouns stacked without concrete referents
- cosmic language unsupported by story
- "the universe" used as an agent
- vague destiny language
- metaphors multiplying faster than the plot
- statements that collapse under literal inspection

A Round should be profound because it is accurate, not because it sounds grand.

## Emotional safety without softness

ROUND ONE is allowed to challenge hard.

But do not encourage self-hatred, imply worth depends on productivity, romanticize self-destruction, shame rest, glorify sleep deprivation, treat injury or illness as weakness, or suggest dangerous behavior as proof of commitment.

The product is about agency, not punishment.

## Final self-test

Before approving a narrative, ask:

1. What happens?
2. Why do I care before I know the lesson?
3. What is the one idea?
4. Where does tension increase?
5. Where does the listener recognize themselves?
6. What changes?
7. Is the realization earned?
8. Is there one line I remember?
9. Does the ending grow naturally from the opening?
10. Would the last minute make someone more likely to move?
