# 14 - Research and Factuality

ROUND ONE may use real people, historical events, science, sports, business, art, and public records.

Truth is part of the product quality.

A powerful story that is false is not a ROUND ONE story.

## Research is required when

A Round includes:
- a real person
- a historical event
- a quotation
- a statistic
- a scientific claim
- a medical or psychological claim
- a specific date
- a specific performance result
- a public anecdote
- a claimed conversation

## Source quality

Prefer primary sources, official records, reputable biographies, direct interviews, high-quality journalism, and authoritative scientific sources.

Use secondary summaries for orientation, not as the sole basis for fragile facts.

## Quotes

Never use a quote because the internet attributes it to someone.

Verify exact wording where possible, speaker, context, and source.

If wording is uncertain, paraphrase and label it as such.

## Dialogue

Do not invent dialogue and present it as fact.

If a real conversation is documented only in paraphrase, narrate it as paraphrase.

## Compression

Narrative compression is allowed only when it does not change meaning.

Do not:
- combine separate events into one scene without disclosure
- move a quote to a different moment
- invent causality
- imply someone knew the future
- turn legend into fact

## Famous-person stories

The audience often already knows the ending.

The job is not to retell the person's whole life.

Find the moment when the outcome was still uncertain.

Preserve uncertainty.

## Scientific ideas

Avoid pop-science exaggeration, causal claims from correlational evidence, neuroscience decoration, "your brain is literally..." language unless literally supported, and using one study as universal human truth.

The story can be simple without making the science false.

## Sensitive topics

When touching grief, mental health, addiction, trauma, illness, suicide, self-harm, or eating disorders, do not turn suffering into a toughness contest.

The product can be intense while remaining humane.

## Research artifact

For any factual Round, retain:
- source list
- key facts
- uncertain facts
- verified quotes
- pronunciation notes
- narrative compression decisions

## Factuality gate

If a key fact cannot be verified and the story depends on it, do not publish the factual version.

Options:
- remove the claim
- rewrite around verified facts
- convert the idea into an explicitly fictional parable if appropriate
- reject the concept

Never choose drama over truth.
